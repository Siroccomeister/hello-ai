# Staying on track — drift and retros

Audience: everyone running any AI-assisted project, especially longer or
open-ended ones. This is the risk message: the goal is easy to lose sight
of, even with good tooling.

## The risk
Long AI conversations drift. Even when the conversation gets summarized or
"compacted" to manage length, the summary can subtly reweight toward
whatever the last few exchanges emphasized — and a conversation that keeps
answering "what's the natural next question" without checking against the
original goal will happily walk you down a rabbit hole that has nothing to
do with the mandate you started with.

## The fix is not "better prompting," it's a cadence
You cannot prevent drift by being more careful in the moment — by the time
a conversation is deep in a tangent, it feels perfectly reasonable
step-by-step. The fix is a forced, regular checkpoint that asks one blunt
question against the written mandate: **are we still on track?**

## What this looks like day to day
- Don't wait for a formal sprint boundary if the project doesn't use
  sprints — a "quick retro" can just be a moment where you reread
  `MANDATE.md` and ask whether the last stretch of work actually served it.
- Do this *especially* after a long single session, not just between
  sessions — length is exactly when drift compounds.
- If the answer is "not really," that's not a failure, that's the retro
  doing its job — correct course and keep going.

## Why this is the same discipline as the rest of ATLAS
Mandate gives you the fixed point to check against. State gives you an
honest record of what actually happened, not what you meant to happen.
Retro is the moment those two get compared on purpose, on a schedule,
instead of never.
